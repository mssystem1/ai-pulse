## Visual direction

**Arc navy, icy cyan, warm gold.** Execution receives a muted mint accent and Prediction a restrained violet accent. Clean typography and the PULSE pulse line make the footage feel like a single product story. The footage stays dominant, with gentle reframing for dense interfaces. No unrelated stock images, flashy glitch effects or cartoon transitions are needed.

The 1920 × 1080 design keeps a permanent title area above the footage and a permanent explanation area below it. The footage occupies roughly x=160–1760, y=162–930; its original aspect ratio is preserved. The title has its own band, and descriptions use at most two lines. Typography is scaled for the final canvas, rather than written into the original screenshots. PULSE's existing Syne / IBM Plex Mono font direction can be used in the render; the offline storyboard uses local system fallbacks.

**Highlights use gradients, not annotation boxes.** A soft radial pool guides attention to the selected field, chart or balance. A subtle light sweep follows a meaningful action, and a 1.05–1.35× eased push-in adds motion. For small wallet values and dense controls, use a separate 1.7–2.2× detail reframe, then return to the wide view so the viewer retains context. Highlights stay light enough to preserve UI text, amount, recipient, route and status. Keep one primary focus at a time.

Short on-video callouts are limited to three to six words, set with a soft shadow and slightly translucent type. They appear only in verified empty space. If a shot has no safe empty region, use the explanation band below the video instead. No callout may cover an amount, wallet confirmation, active button, chart label or error/outcome. The static boards illustrate the treatment; callout placement must be checked again against every moving frame during assembly.

## Transitions and pacing

Use 0.4–0.7-second eased dissolves or a restrained light sweep at major chapter changes. Match shared UI elements where possible: Portfolio to its funding control, report chart to Spot ticket, prediction scenario to strategy choice, journal back to overview. The viewer should understand why the next scene follows. Short within-scene cuts preserve cursor/action direction and the sequence of approvals.

A proposed **120-BPM** score gives two-second bars; all eight chapter boundaries align to bars. Once the exact track is selected, confirm the downbeats and adapt the music edit rather than forcing every UI click to a beat. Essential numbers, wallet review and outcomes get reading holds. Remove repeated scrolling and long generation waits; use the genuine returned report as the next cut. Source ranges are inspected candidates, with final trim points checked at the actual click/confirmation.

## Music and female voice

Instrumental progressive electronic / cinematic synth: warm controlled sub-bass, crisp percussion, airy synths, and a memorable pulse motif. Start with a spare rhythmic hook, build through research and execution, open the arrangement for Autopilot, then resolve cleanly for the last six-second URL hold. The music should energize the film while keeping the product explanation easy to follow.

Include an auditioned **warm, natural adult female English voice**, articulate and confident at approximately **120–130 words per minute**. Avoid a flat robotic delivery or an exaggerated commercial read. Short pauses let the user read each important action. Lower the music beneath narration and restore it between phrases and chapters. The captions remain complete, so the film also works muted or with music alone. The voice and exact music track are not generated/selected in this planning package; check sound quality and music usage rights before the final export.

The proposed narration totals 387 words.

The sources total **344.35 seconds**. Timestamped contact sheets and additional original-resolution frames were inspected before choosing this structure.

- The Spot demonstration is a **WETH/USDC limit order**, based on ETH-USDT research. It is pending before owner cancellation. Do not call it a completed Market buy or filled limit order.
- The running BTC Autopilot has **zero confirmed fills**. The separate paused ETH vault has historical buy/sell records. Preserve vault identity and do not attribute those fills to the running BTC vault.
- Historical dependency journal rows are present. The later dashboard shows dependency recovery. Holds and recovery are decisions and checks, not new trades; the edit must preserve this distinction.
- Gateway contains an actual deposit approval, confirmation and updated balance. A Withdraw control is visible, but the recording does not show a completed withdrawal.
- Prediction demonstrates research, not an executed prediction-market bet.
- Risk Guard is present in the landing overview only. There is no supplied Risk Guard workflow, so its role can be introduced briefly without fabricating a scan.
- No ROI promises, fabricated chart data, edited balances or synthetic successful transactions.

## Final render checks

1. Assemble and audition a short opening/Gateway/Spot sample before rendering all four minutes. Match the voice to the music and check that the explanation is readable with the sound muted.
2. Preserve the original recordings and render from their full resolution, not the reduced inspection thumbnails. Keep original UI values and results intact.
3. Inspect every title, description, animated crop and highlight for overlap, clipping and readable contrast. Complete captions must fit in the reserved lower band.
4. Confirm market identity, action order and the final shown state of each order/vault. Hold the URL clearly for at least six seconds.
5. Export a **1920 × 1080, 30-fps, exactly 240-second MP4**, H.264 with stereo AAC. Check for missing frames, black transitions, distorted audio and excessive sharpening.
6. Watch the complete exported film and spot-check on a phone. Any failed real action must retain its meaning; no publication or upload is part of this local production step.
